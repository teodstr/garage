# Landing page

A landing page is often the first real experiment of a startup: if strangers will not leave an email, they are unlikely to pay. This skill writes the page, builds it as a single HTML file, and sets the numbers that decide what happens next.

## When to use

- To test demand before building (a smoke test), to collect a waitlist, or to take pre-orders.
- After `brand-identity` if a name and colors exist, but a plain page works too.
- Not for a full website or a search engine strategy.

## Inputs

1. Target visitor, problem, promise and any real proof (quotes, numbers, logos with permission).
2. The one action wanted: an email, a pre-order, a demo request.
3. Brand elements if they exist, and where the form data should go.

## Method

1. **One page, one visitor, one action.** Choose the target visitor and the single call to action; remove links that lead away from it.
2. **Write the top of the page first**: a headline that states the result for the visitor (not the technology), a line that says how, the call to action, and an image of the product or of the result. Test: a stranger understands what it is and who it is for in five seconds.
3. **Then the rest**: the problem in the customers' own words (from `customer-interviews`), how it works in three steps, real proof, answers to the main objections (price, trust, time), a short FAQ, and the call to action again.
4. **Stay honest**: if the product does not exist yet, say "join the waitlist" or "pre-order, delivery planned for…". Never invent testimonials, counters or scarcity. For pre-orders, state the delivery date and the refund terms.
5. **Build it as one HTML file**: semantic headings, alternative text on images, readable contrast, works on a phone. The form posts to a service the founder chooses (a form tool, a newsletter tool or a spreadsheet), and a short privacy notice says how the emails will be used (GDPR in the European Union).
6. **Plan the test**: where the visitors will come from (it matters as much as the page), how many, the target conversion decided in advance, and the duration. Test two headlines at most.
7. **Read the results**: visitors and conversion by source, questions people asked, and the decision against the criteria set before launch.

## Output

```markdown
### Page plan
Visitor: … | Promise: … | Call to action: … | Success if …% of … visitors from … within … days

### Copy
Headline: … | Line under it: … | Button: …
Sections: problem, how it works, proof, objections, FAQ

### HTML
<one accessible file, form address to configure, privacy notice included>
```

## Quality bar

- The headline states a result for a specific visitor, and the page has one main call to action.
- Every claim, testimonial and number on the page is real; nothing fakes scarcity or social proof.
- The page is accessible, works on a phone, and explains how collected data is used.
- The traffic plan and the success criterion are written before launch.
- Written in the user's language, with the terms below.
