# Brand identity

A young brand has to be clear and easy to remember before it has to be beautiful. This skill starts from the positioning, proposes names that can be protected, and produces a simple visual direction the founder can use on a landing page this week.

## When to use

- Once the target and the promise are known, usually after the first validation.
- Before building a `landing-page` or a `pitch-deck`.
- Not too early: a name and a logo do not test demand (`startup-roadmap` flags it).

## Inputs

1. Target customer, problem, promise and three competitors.
2. Three to five words that describe the personality the founder wants.
3. Constraints: languages and countries, budget, assets that already exist.

## Method

1. **Write the brand core**: target, promise in one line, proof (why people should believe it), personality in three adjectives, and what the brand is not.
2. **Generate names** in several styles (descriptive, invented, metaphor, compound word): 10 to 15 candidates, then a shortlist of three to five that are short, easy to say and spell in the target languages, and free of unwanted meanings in those languages.
3. **List the checks for each finalist**, and run them when web access is available: trademark databases (INPI in France, OEPM in Spain, EUIPO and TMview for the European Union, the WIPO Global Brand Database worldwide), company registries, domain names and social media handles. A name that looks free in a quick search is not legally cleared: recommend a professional search before investing in it.
4. **Define the voice**: formal or casual (tu or vous, tú or usted), words to use and words to avoid, and two example sentences.
5. **Set a visual direction**: two or three colors with their hex codes, with a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for body text (WCAG level AA); one or two typefaces whose license allows commercial use (for example from Google Fonts); the mood, described in words rather than copied from another brand.
6. **Sketch logos in SVG**: two or three simple concepts (a wordmark, a symbol with a wordmark) that stay readable at 32 pixels and work in one color. Present them as starting points for a designer, not as final logos, and avoid any resemblance to existing marks.
7. **Show it in use**: a mock header of the landing page or a social media profile, so that the founder can judge the identity in context.

## Output

```markdown
### Brand core
Target: … | Promise: … | Proof: … | Personality: … | Not: …

### Name shortlist
| Name | Style | Why it works | Checks to run | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### Voice
…

### Visual direction
Colors: #… (text contrast …:1) | Typefaces: … (license) | Mood: …

### Logo sketches
<two or three inline SVG concepts, readable at 32 px>
```

## Quality bar

- No name is called available or protectable without a real search, and a professional clearance is recommended before filing.
- Text colors pass the WCAG AA contrast ratio; every typeface has a license that allows commercial use.
- Logo sketches are original, simple and readable at small sizes.
- The identity follows from the positioning, not from trends.
- Written in the user's language, with the terms below.
